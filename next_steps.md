# Candidate directions for Phase 2

These are proposals only. **Nothing here has been started**; each needs your review and
approval. All three stay at toy scale and are about mathematical structure. None is a key-recovery
experiment.

## 1. Blind discovery of the endomorphism ring, beyond automorphisms

**Question.** Phase 1's Möbius search re-discovered Aut(E) blindly. Can the same F_p-native
relation search find *every* endomorphism of small degree d? That is, the m with
x_{mk} = R(x_k) for one rational map R of degree d. Is the number found exactly the number of
elements of norm d in End(E)?

**Why it is interesting.** The prediction is concrete and falsifiable from CM theory. Curves
with small CM discriminant (D = −7, −8, −11, −19, …) have endomorphisms of degree 2 or 3, and
the cyclic group gives each one an eigenvalue m mod n. The experiment tests the Phase-1
conjecture R4 in a stronger form. It also connects to GLV-type speedups and to the
Lange–Winterhof follow-up results on curves with degree-2 or degree-3 endomorphisms. A
surprising extra relation would be a genuine, checkable anomaly.

**Plan sketch.**
- Build CM curves for small |D| over 14–17-bit p.
- Generalise the cross-ratio screen to degree-d rational maps, using a nullspace test of
  bidegree (d, 1) on a few points per m, then full verification.
- Compare the counts with the class-number and norm-form prediction.
- Use random curves as controls.
- Bounded at a few minutes.

## 2. Short-segment discrepancy vs proven character-sum bounds

**Question.** For partial periods k ∈ [1, K], how does the empirical discrepancy of x(kG)/p
(1-D, and s-D for consecutive tuples) scale with K and p? How tight are the proven EC-LCG bounds
of Hess–Shparlinski, El Mahassni–Shparlinski and Kohel–Shparlinski, which are typically of
order p^{1/2} log p / K?

**Why it is interesting.** Phase 1 tested only full and half periods and one segment length.
The proven bounds are worst-case and probably far from tight. Measuring the true constant, and
whether j = 0 or supersingular curves differ in *short* segments, is a well-posed
experimental-number-theory question. It could refine conjectures about pseudorandomness of the
EC-LCG. The family comparison also tests whether the "statistically random" picture of Phase 1
survives in the regime where the theory is weakest.

**Plan sketch.**
- K = p^{α} for α ∈ [0.3, 1].
- Several primes from 2^12 to 2^20, at most about 10^6 points per curve.
- Star-discrepancy in dimensions s = 1, 2, 3.
- Compare with random-permutation baselines and with the explicit constants in the cited bounds.
- Pre-register the scaling hypothesis before running.

## 3. The algebraic side: degrees of summation-polynomial systems on toy prime fields

**Question.** Phase 1 established that the only exact relations among x-coordinates are those
generated by the group law: summation polynomials, torsion and automorphisms. The natural next
structural question is how the *algebraic complexity* of these relations behaves at toy sizes.
Examples: the degree of regularity, or the first-fall degree, of the systems S_m(x_1, …, x_{m−1},
X) = 0 with x_i restricted to a small subspace or interval, as m and p vary.

**Why it is interesting, and why it needs care.** This is the established bridge between
"structure" and computational hardness (Semaev; Gaudry; Diem; Petit–Quisquater and others). The
literature says the prime-field case is the *hard* one, and the open question is whether
Gröbner-basis degree behaves as heuristics predict. It is also the direction closest to the DLP,
so **it should be framed purely as measuring algebraic degrees and Gröbner statistics at toy
scale**, with no attempt at solving discrete logarithms. We would need an additional dependency
(a Gröbner-basis engine such as sympy, Singular or msolve), and you should explicitly approve
both the scope and the tooling before we start.

**Plan sketch.**
- Compute S4 and S5 explicitly.
- Measure the Gröbner degree of regularity on random restricted instances for p up to about 2^12.
- Compare with the heuristic first-fall-degree predictions from the literature.
- Report negative results as such.

---

**Recommendation:** start with direction 1. It is the most self-contained, builds directly on
Phase 1's verified tools, has a sharp theoretical prediction, and is the most likely to produce a
clearly interpretable result, positive or negative.

# Status after Phase 2 (direction 1 completed) and candidate Phase-3 directions

Direction 1 is done; see `findings_phase2.md`. It confirmed claim C2, blind recovery of
End(E), in the regime d ≪ q. It also produced one unexpected, apparently curve-independent
regularity, **conjecture C3**, which held on 1454/1454 held-out multipliers. Directions 2 and 3
above remain available. New candidates, none started:

## A. Explain or refute conjecture C3 (recommended)

**Literature, first (needs network access to primary sources).** Read Lange–Winterhof (AAECC
2003) and the WCC 2005 paper on interpolation degrees, then the literature on Fricke functions,
modular units and gonality of X₁(q). C3 may already be known in another language.

**Characteristic-independence test.** If C3 is an identity on the universal curve over X₁(q),
the d_min profile should be the same for every p and in characteristic 0. Two cheap tests:
- many primes p for one q, including p < q and p ≡ 1 (mod q);
- a multi-modular computation over Q(X₁(q)) using the Tate normal form, for q ≤ 31.
A p-dependent exception would refute the universality explanation.

**Proof attempt.** Count zeros and poles of the candidate relation on the universal curve. Test
whether the cusps of X₁(q), labelled by a/b, give the formula ((b−1)q + 4a² − ε_b)/(4b).

## B. Blind detection of End(E) beyond class number one

Use CM curves whose j-invariant is a root of a Hilbert class polynomial (h(D) > 1), and
non-cyclic E(F_p). Compare with isogeny-volcano computations of End(E) (Kohel;
Bisson–Sutherland). This tests C2 where End(E) is not determined by j alone.

## C. Map the C2/C3 boundary for larger q

The (d, 1) screen is cheap. For q ~ 10³–10⁴, measure the first non-endomorphism degree and
compare with C3's lower bound of about (q − 3)/6. This determines whether C3 stays exact, or
other mechanisms enter, at sizes where exhaustive binary search is impractical.

Recommendation: **A**. It is the only Phase-2 output that may be new mathematics, and its first
step (the literature) is cheap but needs the network policy relaxed for arxiv.org, link.springer.com and similar hosts.

# Revised recommendation (post-Phase-2 reflection)

New evidence since Phase 2: the j-table was verified by PARI and Wolfram; C3 holds in
characteristic 0 (numerically, two lattices, q ≤ 43); and C3 is genuinely elliptic, since the
nodal analogue follows a different law. See `research_log.md`, "Post-Phase-2 reflection checks".

1. **Turn C3 into theorems, one q at a time, then look for the general proof.**
   For fixed q, C3 is a statement about level-q modular functions ℘(k/q; τ), so it can be
   decided by finite computation:
   - **Lower bound:** one full-rank specialisation, as in our F_p runs, proves that no
     smaller-degree relation exists generically.
   - **Existence:** show the relevant minors vanish identically on X₁(q), using q-expansions to
     a Sturm-type bound.
   - **Tools:** PARI is already installed; Sage via conda-forge is reachable.
   - **First target:** q = 13, the first prime where C3 predicts a relation below threshold
     (m = −1/3, d = 2).
   - **Literature, still blocked:** Lange–Winterhof (AAECC 2003) and the WCC 2005 interpolation
     paper; Kiltz–Winterhof (2004); Fricke functions and modular units. C3 gives *exact*
     rational interpolation degrees for x(P) ↦ x(mP) on a subgroup, the object those papers
     bound from below.
2. **Generalise C3 carefully:**
   - composite q;
   - Y-degree e > 1, i.e. bidegree (d, e);
   - CM curves, where the conjectured version replaces a/b by α/β with norms;
   - the C2/C3 crossover for q ~ 10³ (direction C).
3. **C2 beyond class number one** (Hilbert class polynomials via `pari.polclass`) is cheap and
   tidy, but mostly confirms known theory.
4. **Deprioritise** statistical pattern-mining of coordinates and anything framed as DLP or
   secp256k1 weakness. Phases 1–2 found nothing of that kind, and theory explains why.
